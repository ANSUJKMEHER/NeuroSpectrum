Refined Research Proposal
Learning Continuous Spectral Interaction Laws for
Controllable Point Distributions
Research direction: retain the working neural-energy prototype, but refine the scientific contribution around continuous
spectral conditioning, unseen-target generalization, learned interaction laws, spatial regularity, amortized efficiency, and
progressive scalability.
1. Executive Summary
The current prototype learns a pairwise neural interaction energy conditioned on a target spectrum, obtains particle forces
by differentiating that energy, evolves particles, rasterizes the final point set, computes its FFT/PSD, and trains through the
spectral objective. This is an excellent foundation, but the basic mechanism alone should not be presented as the main
novelty: neural interaction-energy and learned force-field constructions already have precedent.
The refined research question is stronger: Can one conditional neural energy field learn a continuous family of
particle interaction laws that generates point distributions for unseen target spectra, preserves measurable spatial
regularity, exposes interpretable interaction behavior, and reduces the cost of producing new target distributions
compared with optimizing every target from scratch?
2. Research Problem
Point distributions are important in rendering, sampling, stippling, halftoning, and procedural graphics. Their spatial
behavior can be characterized using power spectral density. Classical optimization can target a desired PSD, but normally
requires a new optimization process for each target. The proposed method instead learns an interaction law once and
conditions it on the desired spectral parameter ╬│.
The central distinction is therefore amortized conditional generation, rather than simply proving that a neural network
can fit one spectrum.
3. Novelty Position
What is not sufficient as the primary novelty
ΓÇó An MLP representing a pairwise energy.
ΓÇó Computing force as the negative gradient of the energy.
ΓÇó Backpropagating through unrolled particle dynamics.
ΓÇó Optimizing a rasterized PSD loss alone.
What should become the research contribution
ΓÇó Continuous spectral interaction learning: a single model represents a family of interaction laws across ╬│.
ΓÇó Unseen-spectrum generalization: evaluate ╬│ values not explicitly used during training.
ΓÇó Interaction-law interpretation: analyze E(r,╬│) and F(r,╬│) rather than treating the network as a black box.
ΓÇó Learned regularity: investigate whether appropriate short-range behavior emerges from training.
ΓÇó Amortized efficiency: compare neural generation for new targets with classical per-target optimization.
ΓÇó Progressive generalization: extend to local, anisotropic, and spatially varying spectra only after the core result is
established.

6. Most Important Refinement: Continuous ╬│
Instead of training only on discrete blue/white/red-like categories, use ╬│ as a continuous control variable. A practical starting
range is ╬│ Γêê [ΓêÆ2,+2]. Random ╬│ sampling during training is preferable to only five fixed values.
ΓÇó Train over a continuous/random distribution of ╬│.
ΓÇó Hold out intermediate values such as ΓêÆ1.5, ΓêÆ0.7, 0.3, 0.8, and +1.5.
ΓÇó Plot target ╬│ against achieved spectral slope and PSD error.
ΓÇó Measure whether spatial statistics change smoothly with ╬│.
ΓÇó Test extrapolation separately from interpolation.
This experiment is the clearest way to show that the network learns a conditional interaction family rather than memorizing
a small set of spectral classes.
7. Loss Function
Use a minimum combined objective:
L = ╬╗PSD Llog-PSD + ╬╗slope Lslope + ╬╗NND LCV-NND
ΓÇó Log-PSD loss: measures spectral fidelity while reducing domination by large spectral magnitudes.
ΓÇó Slope loss: directly measures the target spectral trend.
ΓÇó NND coefficient of variation: discourages pathological particle clustering.
The regularity term is important because differentiable Gaussian-splat rasterization can hide sub-kernel particle collapse
from a purely image/PSD-based loss.
8. Learned Regularity Study
Run three controlled versions:
ΓÇó A ΓÇö spectral loss only.
ΓÇó B ΓÇö spectral + nearest-neighbor regularity.
ΓÇó C ΓÇö spectral + regularity + fixed hand-designed repulsion.
Compare PSD error, pair-correlation g(r), NND CV, discrepancy/Ripley statistics, visual quality, and stability. If B learns
sensible short-range behavior without fixed repulsion, that is stronger evidence that the energy field has learned useful
interaction structure.

10. Required Classical Baseline
Implement direct per-target classical particle-position optimization using the same domain, particle count, spectral
objective, rasterization, FFT resolution, boundary handling, and regularity measures wherever possible.
ΓÇó Measure final PSD/slope error.
ΓÇó Measure spatial regularity.
ΓÇó Measure optimization time for every new ╬│.
ΓÇó Measure neural inference time for new ╬│ after training.
Do not claim the neural approach is better simply because it works. The important comparison is quality, generalization,
and amortized cost.
11. Dynamics and Stability
Begin with Euler or semi-implicit integration and approximately 50ΓÇô100 unrolled steps. Monitor total energy, force norms,
minimum spacing, loss, and displacement.
For ideal continuous gradient flow dX/dt = ΓêÆΓêçE, dE/dt = ΓêÆ||ΓêçE||┬▓ Γëñ 0. This does not by itself establish global convergence for
the complete learned, discretized system, so the paper should report empirical stability rather than overclaim convergence.
12. Scalability
Full pairwise interaction is O(N┬▓). Start with N=256 for validation. Then test larger point sets and introduce
k-nearest-neighbor interactions with k=16, 32, and 64.
ΓÇó Compare runtime and quality.
ΓÇó Test whether local interactions preserve the learned spectrum.
ΓÇó Target N=1,024 and N=4,096 or larger when resources permit.
13. Advanced Extension: Anisotropic Spectra
After isotropic control is reliable, condition on c=[╬│,╬▒,╬╕] and use the relative vector rather than distance alone:
Eij = MLP╬╕(Γêåxij, c)
This allows non-radially-symmetric target PSDs and provides a stronger demonstration of conditional interaction learning.
14. Advanced Extension: Spatially Varying Spectra
Use a spatial condition ╬│(x,y) so different regions of one domain request different spectral behavior. A compelling
demonstration is a smooth red-like ΓåÆ white-like ΓåÆ blue-like transition in one point distribution. This should be attempted
only after the global scalar-╬│ model is strong.
15. Evaluation Protocol
ΓÇó Spectral: log-PSD MSE, slope error, frequency-band error.
ΓÇó Spatial: pair-correlation g(r), NND CV, discrepancy, and/or Ripley's K/L.
ΓÇó Generalization: unseen interpolation and separate extrapolation tests.
ΓÇó Efficiency: training cost, new-target generation time, classical per-target optimization time.
ΓÇó Robustness: at least three random seeds, initialization changes, particle-count changes, and boundary variations.
ΓÇó Visual: consistent point rendering plus at least one graphics/rendering application.

20. Risk Management
ΓÇó Classical method is equally good: emphasize continuous generalization, interaction interpretability, or amortized cost
only if quantitatively supported.
ΓÇó Particle collapse: inspect NND statistics; add regularity loss or a minimal stabilizer as an explicit ablation.
ΓÇó BPTT memory: shorten unrolls, checkpoint, or move to kNN.
ΓÇó Rasterization artifacts: validate gradients and vary kernel size/resolution.
ΓÇó Memorization: use random ╬│ training and strict held-out ╬│ tests.
ΓÇó Uncertain novelty: complete a targeted literature review before final publication claims.

22. Recommended Paper Narrative
ΓÇó Different target spectra imply different desired spatial interaction behavior.
ΓÇó A conditional neural energy can represent this behavior as a continuous interaction family.
ΓÇó Unseen target spectra can be generated without training a separate model for each target.
ΓÇó Energy/force plots reveal how the learned interaction law changes with spectral condition.
ΓÇó Spatial statistics verify that spectral matching does not hide pathological clustering.
ΓÇó A classical baseline establishes whether amortized learning is practically useful.
ΓÇó Local interactions establish scalability.
ΓÇó Anisotropic and spatially varying spectra provide progressive extensions.
23. Refined Research Claim
This work proposes learning a continuous family of particle interaction laws conditioned on a desired spatial spectrum. The
objective is not merely to generate a few predefined spectral point sets, but to learn an interaction field that generalizes to
unseen and intermediate targets, exhibits interpretable changes in energy and force with the spectral condition, preserves
measurable spatial regularity, and amortizes the cost of generating new distributions. The method will be evaluated against
direct classical per-target optimization and progressively extended to scalable local interactions, anisotropic spectra, and
spatially varying spectral control.
24. Final Success Criterion
The project becomes a strong research contribution only if three results are simultaneously demonstrated: (1)
quantitative unseen-spectrum generalization, (2) meaningful interpretation of the learned interaction law, and (3) a
defensible quality/efficiency advantage or other clear benefit over classical per-target optimization. The current working
prototype should therefore be retained as the foundation, while these experiments provide the refinement that turns it into a
research study.
